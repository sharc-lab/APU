\title{Silent by Default: How Runtime Policy and Memory Architecture Break Local LLM Agents on AI PCs}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Author(s)}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Submission under double-blind review}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Local LLM agents on AI PCs rely on a runtime to choose the context window and manage memory. We measure what happens when either runs out, with pre-registered experiments on an AMD APU, an NVIDIA laptop GPU and two Intel machines. First, the failure is silent. When a prompt outgrows the window, Ollama keeps about half and reports success% reg: ollama-overflow-keeps-half
. On tool-using tasks, qwen3:14b ended 42.0\% (63/150) of tasks wrong at a 4096-token window against 11.3\% (17/150) at its default% reg: x2-evalset-tasks-real-v2-wrong-rate
, and qwen3:8b 80.4\% (193/240) of turns at its 4096-token default against 25.0\% (60/240) at 24576% reg: pkg1109-t4-8b
, every wrong answer at 4096 silent. Second, the device and runtime set the window: Ollama loaded 4096 tokens for llama3.1:8b on one machine and 131072 on another% reg: T2S-vs-X2-default-ctx
, and serving stacks answer an over-length prompt with silent truncation or an HTTP error% reg: pkg1109-contract1 | xruntime-llamacpp-wrong-rate
. Third, the memory figures runtimes report miss the real limit. On the AMD APU, spill began past the 65536 MiB dedicated block, inside the advertised 76218 MiB heap% reg: fh1-amd-boundary-llama-3.3-70b
; on an Intel laptop, lowering the GPU memory share did not move the paging onset% reg: pkg1109-w3-50 | pkg1109-x1
, and free memory after load overstated usable memory by a median 1,238 MiB (99 probes, exploratory)% reg: pkg1109-os-reserve
. No spill or paging point raised an error% reg: fh1-amd-boundary-llama-3.3-70b | pkg1109-x1
.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:4.1}

% Convention as in 4.3: measured numbers carry their register id; design constants carry "design: <source file>".

Runtimes such as Ollama and llama.cpp serve open-weight models of 8B to 70B parameters on laptops and small APU workstations, and local agents drive them for tens of turns, appending every tool result to a conversation that grows inside a fixed context window. When it outgrows the window, the runtime decides what to remove. On Ollama this is silent: the request returns HTTP 200 % reg: blade-k1-layer-split
with a well-formed answer and no field reporting the loss; llama-server at the same window fails loudly, with HTTP errors % reg: xruntime-llamacpp-wrong-rate
(Fig.~\ref{fig:FW}).

\textbf{Why run locally, and what it costs.} Priced on public agent traces (not executed; token counts recalibrated to the cloud tokenizer), a median long-horizon coding-agent task costs USD 2.5307 on a mid-tier cloud model and USD 0.1354 on a small one with realistic caching% reg: cloud-cost-per-real-task
. Our own tool-using tasks are short: in the cloud the small and mid-tier models were correct on 87/90 and 90/90 % reg: all-cloud-b3-quality
at a mean USD 0.00067 and USD 0.01020 per task% reg: all-cloud-b3-cost
. Run locally on qwen3:14b at its default window, the bill disappears, but 10 of 90 tasks were wrong and nothing marked them% reg: hybrid-savings-v1
; a perfect detector would reach 90/90 by escalating those 10% reg: hybrid-savings-v1
, but no such detector exists (Section~\ref{sec:4.7}).

The window is often not the user's choice: on an Intel mini PC, Ollama's device detection drops the integrated GPU% reg: x2-device-detect-mechanism
, so an unmodified install served two models with a 4096 % reg: t2s-r2-real-cpu
token window and every agent session failed silently (Section~\ref{sec:4.5}).

We ask what a local agent loses when its conversation outgrows the window or its memory, whether anything tells the user, and what decides how often it happens, with pre-registered experiments on an AMD APU, an NVIDIA laptop GPU and two Intel machines (a mini PC and a laptop), spanning unified and discrete memory. We make four contributions.

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Where memory runs out, and how} (Section~\ref{sec:4.4}). The AMD APU spills past its dedicated block, not its advertised heap, at almost no cost% reg: fh1-amd-boundary-llama-3.3-70b
; on the Intel mini PC's iGPU a first-principles accounting predicts the start-or-refuse flip point to within -257 to -26 tokens% reg: A-24-flip-point-prediction
, while on an Intel laptop the same kind of accounting misses the silent paging onset (0 of 3)% reg: pkg1109-x1
.

\item \textbf{The runtime and serving stack set the window and the overflow behaviour} (Section~\ref{sec:4.5}). Ollama gives llama3.1:8b 4096 tokens on the Intel mini PC and 131072 on the AMD APU % reg: T2S-vs-X2-default-ctx
and answers an over-length prompt with success after keeping num\_ctx/2 + 2 tokens% reg: ollama-overflow-keeps-half
; other stacks reject it or, like OpenVINO (provisionally), also run silently past the window% reg: pkg1109-contract1
.

\item \textbf{The consequences for agents, with their mechanism} (Section~\ref{sec:4.6}). History loss is silent in every model tested, 8B to 70B% reg: H-scale-verdict | H-scale-family-verdict
; on two task workloads a 4096-token window more than triples wrong task end states, all silent on Ollama; at 4096 a context shift and a token cut discard the system prompt% reg: R2-mechanism-lowlevel
.

\item \textbf{What to set, and what still fails} (Sections~\ref{sec:4.7} and \ref{sec:4.7p}). Set the window per device in a Modelfile and check the loaded value% reg: window-override-methods
, prefer a quantized KV cache to a smaller window% reg: b5-policy-metrics
, and set \texttt{num\_keep} to the system prompt's length% reg: num-keep-guardrail-test
. History is still lost at small windows% reg: num-keep-guardrail-test
, trimming still fails qwen3:14b% reg: R2-mitigation-v1-verdict | t2s-r2-mitigation-cpu
, and no check we tested finds the silent wrong answers; the runtime's shift rule matches every shift (misses 0% reg: R2-shift-predictor-confusion
) but not them.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:4.2}

% Citations below were checked against their sources on 2026-11-13 (bib: docs/paper1/references.bib). A citation still marked "to verify" has an open question listed under References.

\textbf{Context windows in local runtimes.} llama.cpp allocates a KV cache for n\_ctx tokens when a model loads. When a sequence would grow past it, the server can perform a \emph{context shift}: keep the first n\_keep tokens, discard a block after them and continue. Ollama serves models through a bundled llama.cpp server and adds two layers: its chat endpoint drops whole messages from the front of an over-length conversation while keeping the system message% reg: R2-mechanism-verdict
, and it starts the server with context shift on and a keep of 4, so an over-length prompt succeeds after keeping num\_ctx/2 + 2 tokens% reg: ollama-overflow-keeps-half
; the option \texttt{num\_keep} sets the kept prefix. Without a client \texttt{num\_ctx}, Ollama picks the window from the detected device. None of this is reported.

\textbf{Long-context behaviour.} Lost in the Middle~\cite{liu2024lost} finds the middle of a long input used worse than its ends; RULER~\cite{hsieh2024ruler} finds effective context shorter than the advertised window; LongBench~\cite{bai2024longbench} notes that most LLMs ``can only handle texts a few thousand tokens long'' and finds that GPT-3.5-Turbo-16k ``still struggles on longer contexts''; and multi-turn~\cite{laban2025lost} and rule-following benchmarks~\cite{zhou2023ifeval}~\cite{mu2023rules} measure how well a model uses or obeys its input. All assume the model receives the whole input.

\textbf{KV-cache management.} StreamingLLM~\cite{xiao2024streamingllm} keeps initial attention-sink tokens plus a recent window, much as a context shift keeps n\_keep tokens; H2O~\cite{zhang2023h2o} and SnapKV~\cite{li2024snapkv} evict by attention; KIVI~\cite{liu2024kivi} and KVQuant~\cite{hooper2024kvquant} quantize the cache; LLMLingua~\cite{jiang2023llmlingua} compresses the prompt. We measure what shipped runtimes discard by default.

\textbf{Agents, routing and delegation.} AgentBench~\cite{liu2024agentbench}, tau-bench~\cite{yao2024taubench}, function-calling evaluations~\cite{patil2024gorilla} and SWE-bench~\cite{jimenez2024swebench} score task success, usually with hosted models and large windows. RouteLLM~\cite{routellm} learns from preference data the probability that the strong model wins and routes on it, Hybrid LLM~\cite{ding2024hybrid} routes on predicted query difficulty, cascades~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt} escalate when a scorer distrusts the cheap answer, and self-verification~\cite{weng2023selfverification} and sampling-consistency checks~\cite{manakul2023selfcheckgpt} let a model test its own answer; all need a signal that correlates with a wrong answer. EdgeWise~\cite{shi2026edgewise} learns which steps of a long agent task to delegate to the cloud, EC2-Bench~\cite{huang2026ec2bench} benchmarks edge-cloud collaboration, Minions~\cite{narayan2025minions} has a cloud model decompose tasks for an on-device model, Arch-Router~\cite{tran2025archrouter} routes by user preferences, and llmfit~\cite{llmfit} estimates which models fit a machine's memory. None measures how the window a runtime loads changes an agent's correctness.

\textbf{Silent truncation is known; its rate, cause and device dependence are not.} An issue open since 2024 asks Ollama to report truncation from exceeding the context size~\cite{ollama3839}. A 2026 issue~\cite{ollama14259} documents that chat-history truncation is silent and that its only log line is at debug level (\texttt{slog.Debug}), invisible at the default level; a comment reports the same on v0.34.0 with ROCm. A practitioner write-up~\cite{desbiens2026contextpaw} shows Ollama returning success after evaluating 16,387 tokens of a 160,689-token prompt at \texttt{num\_ctx} 32768, the model confabulating the lost fact, while llama-server at a 4096 window rejected an over-length request with \texttt{exceed\_context\_size\_error}; with only a marker saying content was gone, its compaction recovered 0 of 3 facts. These are single cases, without rates, device dependence or memory analysis; we add measured rates on agent tasks, the device-dependent default windows and the mechanism.

\textbf{Inference on edge hardware.} PagedAttention~\cite{kwon2023vllm} manages KV memory on servers; MELT~\cite{laskaridis2024melt}, PowerInfer~\cite{song2024powerinfer} and LLM in a flash~\cite{edge_llm_serving} characterize or accelerate inference on phones, consumer GPUs and flash-backed devices, not behavioural correctness under a runtime's memory and context policy.

\section{Platforms, Harness and Methodology}\label{sec:4.3}

% Convention: measured numbers carry their register id; harness design constants carry "design: <source file>".

\textbf{Platforms and runtimes.} We study four consumer machines from three GPU vendors (Table~\ref{tab:platforms}): an AMD Ryzen AI Max+ (Strix Halo) APU whose Radeon iGPU shares unified memory with the CPU; a laptop with an NVIDIA RTX 4070 Laptop GPU and 8188 MiB % reg: blade-k1-layer-split
of VRAM; an Intel Core Ultra (Panther Lake) mini PC with an Arc iGPU on unified memory; and an Intel laptop whose iGPU draws on a configurable share of system memory (Sections~\ref{sec:4.4} and \ref{sec:4.5}). Ollama's device detection drops the Intel iGPU and falls back to the CPU% reg: x2-device-detect-mechanism
, so we measure the mini PC twice: on the CPU, as shipped, and on the iGPU, enabled only for the job's own server process. All agent runs use Ollama; the AMD and NVIDIA runs share one pinned release and the model files.

\textbf{The R2 agent harness.} A session is a seeded, deterministic agent conversation of 40 turns% reg: R2-v1b-qwen3-8b-reversal
. The system prompt carries five % reg: design: harness/x2_r2_agent.py
checkable rules (JSON answer with fixed keys, call a \texttt{log\_event} tool, never print a forbidden token, lengths in metres, end with a session code), a two-tool schema and recall facts. Each turn is two % reg: design: harness/x2_r2_agent.py
calls: the first returns tool calls, answered with a long filler payload that grows the context as real tool output does; the second asks for the final answer, at temperature 0% reg: design: harness/x2_r2_agent.py
. Canaries in the system prompt and history tags in the first user message detect lost context; each canary turn asks for a new pair% reg: R2-validation-baseline
. A miss counts as truncation only while the final prompt exceeds the context Ollama reports as loaded (\texttt{/api/ps}, read every turn). R2 runs withhold tools on the final call; Section~\ref{sec:4.6a} repeats the main contrast with tools on every call.

\textbf{Validation gates.} Each model first passes a validation, with gates fixed in advance, at a window where nothing is truncated. A rule is \emph{in use} for a model only if the model follows it on at least 90\% % reg: x2-evalset-val-v2-gates
of baseline turns; rules below the bar are dropped, not repaired. For llama3.1:8b only rules 1, 3 and 4 % reg: R2-validation-v2-baseline
are in use; qwen3:14b kept all five. A real job refuses to start unless its model's validation passes.

\textbf{Gated silent failure.} For each session we take the first turn on which a gated metric fails. The failure is \emph{silent} when no HTTP error or error field appeared on any earlier turn, and \emph{after-window} when it falls at or after the first turn whose prompt exceeded the loaded window; otherwise it is \emph{baseline-type}. The window effect is the \emph{excess over default}: a tier's after-window rate minus the Ollama default tier's, with a 95\% percentile bootstrap CI over sessions (10000 % reg: R2-v1b-excess-over-default
resamples, stratified by tier and model); where both cells are 0/n or n/n we report a one-sided Fisher exact test over sessions instead% reg: session-exact-tests
.

\textbf{Pre-registration and provenance.} The R2 kill criterion was written before any session ran: the claim fails if, in every arm, the first failure comes only after an explicit error in the same session. The gated definition came later, as analysis, and is reported beside the criterion. Every follow-up was pre-registered (hypothesis, verdict rule and validity conditions committed before deployment), and we report verdicts in that wording, refutations included. The second task workload, the Intel laptop and the serving-stack probes use a separate harness whose predictions were sealed before each run; we report its verdicts as sealed, misses included. Every number is pasted from a numbers register that one script regenerates from the raw result files. Runs before 2026-10-14 rest on byte-identical reruns for their harness identity (identical final text 120/120% reg: R2-qwen3-14b-4096-reconciliation
). Rows are never pooled across platforms, and a configuration flag counts only when the runtime's log shows it applied.

\section{Memory-Architecture Boundaries}\label{sec:4.4}

\textbf{Claim.} Running out of GPU memory has a different shape on each architecture (Fig.~\ref{fig:FH1}), and on the AMD APU and the Intel laptop the boundary did not follow the memory figure the platform reports.

\textbf{The AMD APU spills past its dedicated block, not its advertised heap, at almost no cost.} We ran llama-3.3-70b and qwen3-32b in llama-server with the Vulkan backend and every layer on the GPU (\texttt{-ngl 99}% reg: fh1-amd-boundary-llama-3.3-70b
), stepping the window and recording per-process GPU memory, decode rate and time to first token (TTFT); a point spills when shared GPU usage grows by more than 1024 MiB over the smallest window% reg: fh1-amd-boundary-llama-3.3-70b
. For llama-3.3-70b spill began between windows 65536 and 100096, where the process held 1.122 of the 65536 MiB dedicated block but only 0.965 of the 76218 MiB device-local heap the driver advertises% reg: fh1-amd-boundary-llama-3.3-70b
, so Fig.~\ref{fig:FH1} normalizes the AMD axis by the dedicated block. Crossing the onset is nearly free, decode ratio 0.983 and TTFT ratio 1.004, and nothing in the response reports the spill% reg: fh1-amd-boundary-llama-3.3-70b
; the server failed only far beyond, past the trained context (Table~\ref{tab:memory}). For qwen3-32b no unclamped spill point was measured: its onset lies above 40960% reg: fh1-amd-boundary-qwen3-32b | fh1-amd-qwen3-32b-spill-sweep
.

\textbf{The Intel mini PC's iGPU has a hard wall that first-principles accounting predicts.} A model either starts or the runtime refuses it, and across five models the refusal point is consistent with one shared-heap limit L in (48112.7, 48124.0] MiB, 247 MiB above the 47866 MiB budget the driver reports% reg: A-24-effective-heap-limit
; the limit counts host-visible buffers as well as device memory (Table~\ref{tab:memory}). Counting weights, KV cache and compute buffers against L, fitted leave-one-model-out, predicts each model's flip point within -257 to -26 tokens, against -2890 to -820 tokens for model file size alone % reg: A-24-flip-point-prediction
(Fig.~\ref{fig:F2}). The flip points range from (23296, 23552] tokens for llama-3.3-70b to (343808, 344064] for llama31-8b, with qwen3-32b at (115712, 115968] and the other two at (305152, 305408] (qwen3-8b) and (320256, 320512] (qwen3-30b-a3b-2507)% reg: A-24-flip-point-per-model
. These are start tests on one driver and boot state; where the flip point lies beyond the trained context it is a memory boundary, not a usable window.

\textbf{The Intel laptop pages silently, and accounting does not predict where.} On the laptop we served Qwen3-4B through OpenVINO on the iGPU. A prompt length is a \emph{paging onset} when the first turn completes but host free memory reaches a fixed floor and prefill runs well above its cold-cache time; we located it by bisection for 16-, 8- and 4-bit KV caches (f16, i8, u4)% reg: pkg1109-x1
. No paging point raised an error, and the allocation per token matched the accounting of KV cache and activations, but all three onset predictions missed, and so did dividing the free memory reported after load by that allocation (0 of 3)% reg: pkg1109-x1
: the allocation is right, the memory it is divided into is not. Lowering the iGPU's share of memory to 50\% did not lower the onset% reg: pkg1109-w3-50
. An exploratory fit finds a reserve of a median 1,238 MiB between the free memory reported after load and what a prompt can use, but it differs by model% reg: pkg1109-os-reserve
, and a sealed per-model predictor of the largest prompt that holds hit 5 of 12 cells% reg: pkg1109-e2
. Paging, not prompt length, causes the break: the 64 GB Intel mini PC held prompts of up to 97,000 tokens that the laptop took 2,913 s to prefill% reg: pkg1109-t1
, and chunked prefill moved the laptop's i8 onset to about 79,000 to 80,000 tokens % reg: pkg1109-c256
(Table~\ref{tab:memory}).

\textbf{On the NVIDIA laptop GPU, Ollama moves layers to the CPU above 8192 tokens.} With 8188 MiB of VRAM% reg: blade-k1-layer-split
, Ollama keeps llama3.1:8b fully on the GPU at windows of 4096 and 8192 and moves layers to the CPU above them, with VRAM shares of 0.870 at 16384 and 0.656 at 32768; for qwen3:8b the shares are 0.805 and 0.592% reg: blade-k1-layer-split
.

\section{Runtime Policy}\label{sec:4.5}

\textbf{Claim.} The runtime's policy for the detected device sets the window, and the serving stack decides what happens past it.

\textbf{Default windows follow the detected device.} Ollama discovers the AMD iGPU through ROCm, so the AMD APU runs on its iGPU, while the Intel mini PC, with no ROCm path, falls through to the CPU% reg: x2-device-detect-mechanism
. On the Intel mini PC the runtime loaded 4096 tokens for each of three models; on the AMD APU it loaded 262144 for qwen3-4b-2507, 131072 for llama3.1:8b and 40960 for qwen3:8b% reg: T2S-vs-X2-default-ctx
, each the model's native window (capped only for qwen3:8b% reg: K1v3-X2-table
). The discrete GPU also got 4096 for both models measured there% reg: blade-k1-layer-split
. The runtime's log reports the rule: a default sized from detected VRAM, 4096 for the discrete GPU's 8.0 GiB and 32768 for the Intel iGPU's 36.2 GiB when that iGPU is enabled % reg: gpu-memory-readonly
(Table~\ref{tab:defaults}, Fig.~\ref{fig:F5}).

\subsection{The Mechanism on AMD and NVIDIA}\label{sec:4.6b}

\textbf{Ollama removes history first and, at 4096, the system prompt.} We reran llama3.1:8b on the AMD APU, one session per tier (80 calls each% reg: R2-mechanism-lowlevel
), with Ollama in debug mode, its log sliced per call, and a render-only copy of each request; the rendered prompt matched the runtime's count on every check (9/9 within 1\% at 4096; render/log disagreements 0% reg: R2-mechanism-verdict
). Ollama drops whole messages from the front once the conversation outgrows the window, on 54/80 calls at 16384, 68/80 at 8192 and 76/80 at 4096% reg: R2-mechanism-lowlevel
, always keeping the system message % reg: R2-mechanism-verdict
and usually cutting mid-turn: history goes, the rules stay in view. At 4096 % reg: R2-mechanism-lowlevel
two lower-level steps then remove the system prompt. llama.cpp's context shift fired on 34 calls from turn 4, each keeping n\_keep 5 tokens and discarding the next 2045, 29 of them on the final-answer call; and once Ollama's own token cut reduced a prompt of 4097 tokens to 2050, keeping 4% reg: R2-mechanism-lowlevel
, the num\_ctx/2 + 2 overflow rule% reg: ollama-overflow-keeps-half
. No step raised an error (exceed-context errors 0% reg: R2-mechanism-verdict
). The hypothesis ``drop old turns, keep the system message'' is therefore confirmed at 32768, 16384 and 8192 and refuted at 4096 by the token cut% reg: R2-mechanism-verdict
.

\textbf{NVIDIA replicates the first two steps.} One session at 4096 on the NVIDIA laptop passed the same render check (9/9 within 1\%); Ollama trimmed whole messages on 75/80 calls from turn 3 and kept the system message on every one, and the context shift fired 33 times; no token cut occurred and no error surfaced% reg: blade-mechanism-4096
. That row lacks n\_keep, so the claim that the shift discards the system prompt is AMD-only.

\textbf{Tool calls written as text, a second silent failure, follow the llama.cpp backend.} In the NVIDIA validation, llama3.1:8b's negative control at 32768, where nothing is truncated, missed canaries in two of five sessions% reg: blade-r2-gate-v2
: the final answer was a tool call written as text. Three pre-registered AMD checks each moved one factor toward the NVIDIA configuration: ROCm at 32768 gave 0 of 20 misses% reg: x2-r2-llama-val-32k
, forcing NVIDIA's CPU offload split (22 of 33 layers on the GPU, VRAM share 0.656) again 0 of 20% reg: x2-r2-llama-val-32k-offload
, and switching only the backend, to Vulkan on the same GPU, 2 of 20, as a text call to \texttt{lookup\_fact}% reg: x2-r2-llama-val-32k-vulkan
. The failure follows the backend (CUDA and Vulkan, not ROCm, in these seeds), not the GPU vendor, but on Vulkan that rests on one session, and the kernel is not identified.

\section{Fixes}\label{sec:4.7}

\textbf{Client-side trimming fixes llama3.1:8b but not qwen3:14b, on two vendors.} Our pre-registered client fix counts each request with the model's tokenizer and drops whole old turns until the system prompt, the kept turns and the answer budget fit the window with a 5\% % reg: design: harness/x2_r2_client_trim.py
margin. A model \emph{recovers} when every rule in use is at or above 90\%% reg: design: analysis/r2_mitigation_report.py
, and the hypothesis needed both models to recover at 4096% reg: R2-mitigation-v1-verdict
. Mechanically it worked: 0 context shifts, 0 token cuts, 0 Ollama message drops and 0 exceed-context errors in every cell% reg: R2-mitigation-v1-observed
. The verdict is \emph{refuted}% reg: R2-mitigation-v1-verdict
: on the AMD APU llama3.1:8b recovered at 8192 and 4096, and qwen3:14b at 8192 but not at 4096% reg: R2-mitigation-v1-verdict
, where its \texttt{log\_event} rule fell to 61.7\% (74/120)% reg: R2-mitigation-v1-4096 | paper-percentage-counts
; the Intel mini PC's CPU replicates the split% reg: t2s-r2-mitigation-cpu
. The cause is the fix itself: at 4096 the budget leaves room for only 1-2 % reg: R2-mitigation-v1-observed
turns besides the system prompt, so trimming removes the examples of the two-call pattern the model imitates: on the first failing trimmed turn qwen3:14b skipped the \texttt{log\_event} call that the untrimmed run made first% reg: R2-qwen3-14b-4096-reconciliation
.

\textbf{num\_keep keeps one model's rules in view, but not its history or task accuracy.} We pre-registered a test for llama3.1:8b at 4096 with \texttt{num\_keep} set to the system prompt's measured length (601, 594 and 597 tokens per seed). All 105 context shifts kept n\_keep 595, 598 or 602, and the rules in use held, which the pre-registered rule reads as ``one-line fix''% reg: num-keep-guardrail-test
. It fixes the rules and nothing else: Ollama still dropped messages on 224 of 239 calls and the history canary was missed on 24 of 24 checks% reg: num-keep-guardrail-test

(Table~\ref{tab:fixes}). What lowered that rate is not established: in the R2 harness the same flag changed nothing for qwen3:14b at 4096% reg: R2-qwen3-14b-4096-reconciliation

\textbf{A cloud router does not dominate.} The eight % reg: design: harness/policy_compare.py
policies were the default, the largest window that starts, client trimming, a cloud router, an auto-sized window, \texttt{num\_keep}, and q8\_0 and q4\_0 KV caches; the hypothesis was that the router is on the Pareto frontier on every platform. It is \emph{refuted}: on the AMD APU the router was dominated for both models% reg: b5-verdict
. Every policy kept every rule (300/300 for llama3.1:8b and 540/540 for qwen3:14b% reg: b5-policy-metrics
), and the router was slower or costlier than the KV-cache policies at the same or higher recall (Section~\ref{sec:4.5}, Table~\ref{tab:fixes}); under the simulated cap it joined the frontier, beside client trimming% reg: b5-sim-constraint
.

\textbf{Recovering after a silent failure is costly and incomplete.} A cloud model given the whole client-side history late, on every wrong task of the protocol check, fixed 84.8\% (167/197) % reg: cloud-redo-full-context
at 1.76x the cost of a fresh run of the same task% reg: late-escalation-cost-vs-fresh
, and it cannot undo what the local model did: of the 30 tasks still wrong, 28 needed a repair of changed state% reg: cloud-redo-full-context
. Rolling back the executed actions first, on simulated tools, fixed all 28 against a pre-registered target of 25/28% reg: own-rollback-redo
, and 195/197 wrong local tasks overall with the full-history redo% reg: cloud-rescue-combined
; replacing earlier turns by reference calls stands in for a successful escalation of each, not a live deployment. Deciding at session start instead, no measured device qualifies at matched quality % reg: hybrid-route-at-start
(Table~\ref{tab:fixes}).

\textbf{No detector we tested finds the wrong tasks.} Every pre-registered attempt to find or avoid the wrong tasks missed its target (Table~\ref{tab:fixes}): a tuned post-run check% reg: post-run-check-tuned
, a session-start fit predictor% reg: fit-predictor-v1
, a route, check and redo pipeline% reg: hybrid-full-pipeline-v1

, and every route-at-start policy% reg: hybrid-route-at-start-v2

\subsection{Predictor}\label{sec:4.7p}

\textbf{The runtime's shift rule predicts every shift.} The shift that discards the system prompt (Section~\ref{sec:4.6b}) has a trigger readable from the llama.cpp source: the prompt left after the message-level trim, plus the tokens generated, exceeds the loaded window. Scored against the shifts the server log recorded in the AMD mechanism sessions, it gave TP 34 FP 0 FN 0 TN 46 at 4096 and TP 10 FP 0 FN 0 TN 70 at 32768 (34/34 and 10/10), misses 0 over all tiers% reg: R2-shift-predictor-confusion
. The generated count is not known before the call; substituting the generation budget, P+num\_predict$>$n\_ctx gives TP 44 FP 22 FN 0% reg: R2-shift-predictor-confusion
, missing no shift at the cost of false alarms; it has no tunable parameter but was scored on the sessions it was built from.

\textbf{It predicts the shift, not the failure.} Applied to the real run as a prediction, the rule flags 102/240 calls for llama3.1:8b at 4096 and 0/240 for qwen3:14b% reg: R2-shift-predicted-real-v1
, yet qwen3:14b also loses rules at 4096, through what the message-level trim removes% reg: R2-qwen3-14b-4096-reconciliation
: at 4096, 80.3\% (61/76) of truncated calls began with an orphaned message% reg: R2-orphan-rates
.

\textbf{Window overflow is not a general wrong-answer detector.} We measured ``this task turn's prompt exceeded the loaded window'' as a predictor of ``the task ended wrong'' on the protocol check (qwen3:14b, AMD APU): it missed every wrong task at the default, where no turn overflowed, and at 4096 it flagged nearly every turn % reg: impact-v3-detection-value-measured
(Table~\ref{tab:fixes}). With a large window wrong answers have other causes; with a small one nearly every step overflows. The shift rule and the window flag explain system-prompt loss; they are not detectors or a cheap escalation filter.

\section{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:4.8}

\textbf{Platforms and versions.} We measure one machine per vendor (two for Intel), so a platform effect cannot be separated from that machine's driver and memory configuration; on the AMD APU an unrequested driver install during one serving-stack step confounds its Vulkan cells (Section~\ref{sec:4.5}). Agent runs use Ollama 0.34.4 % reg: R2-qwen3-14b-4096-reconciliation
(0.40.14 for the second workload% reg: pkg1109-t4-8b
) and direct runs llama.cpp b10970% reg: design: docs/HARDWARE.md (version record; no register row, gap G13)
; default windows and trimming are runtime code and can change in any release. The NVIDIA laptop contributes validation and mechanism sessions only, because its validation gate held back the real agent runs% reg: blade-r2-gate-v2
, and the router comparison ran on the AMD APU alone% reg: b5-verdict
, whose default window is the model's native one.

\textbf{Statistics.} Cells have three % reg: H-scale-verdict
to five % reg: R2-v1b-gated-per-model
seeds, ten for extended cells % reg: R2-qwen3-8b-reversal-10seed
and eight for the second workload% reg: pkg1109-t4-8b
; 0/5 and 5/5 cells carry a session-level Fisher exact test% reg: session-exact-tests
, and the scale and family results rest on three sessions per cell. Sampling uses temperature 0 % reg: design: harness/x2_r2_agent.py
and a fixed request seed, so seeds vary the session script, not the sampling. Timed or scored calls follow one warm-up plus N measured calls (N = 3 for the descriptive KV recall check% reg: b5-kv-recall-f16-rerun
); session times are over three seeds% reg: cost-cut-config-time
.

\textbf{Workload.} The R2 rules are synthetic and checkable; deployed system prompts differ. Guardrail rules 6 to 8 (privacy, refusal, confirmation) already fail at full context, for example rule 6 at 0.417 and 0.250 of bait turns% reg: x2-evalset-val-v1-gates
, so they support no claim here. B3 task accuracy rests on qwen3:14b, which passed the task validation only through a declared exception: it passed every gate except task end state, 80/90 (88.9\%) against a 90\% bar, and an exception declared before its real run waived that gate for exactly that count% reg: x2-evalset-val-v2-gates | x2-evalset-val-v2-task-end-state
. qwen3:8b failed the same validation (task end state 65/90, 72.2\%; task tool 61/81)% reg: x2-evalset-tasks-val-v2-qwen3-8b-gates
, and so did llama3.1:8b, mistral-small3.2:24b and llama3.3:70b% reg: x2-evalset-tasks-val-v2-qwen3-8b-gates | x2-evalset-tasks-real-v2-wrong-rate | x2-evalset-tasks-val-v2-mistral-small32-gates | x2-evalset-tasks-val-v2-llama33-70b-gates
; the second workload adds qwen3:8b on the Intel mini PC under its own harness and validation.

\textbf{Open items.} Still open are the AMD APU's Vulkan cells at 96 GB, to be rerun on a fixed driver% reg: pkg1109-evo-x2-split96
, the cost of crossing the NVIDIA GPU's memory boundary, whose placement alone we measured% reg: blade-k1-layer-split

.

\textbf{Cost figures.} Cloud cost per real agent task is priced from traces, not executed, with caching assumed% reg: cloud-cost-per-real-task

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:4.9}

On every platform we measured, Ollama trims, shifts or cuts a conversation that outgrows the window and still succeeds; llama-server at 4096 fails loudly% reg: xruntime-llamacpp-wrong-rate
. An unmodified Intel CPU install ran agents at 4096 tokens% reg: t2s-r2-real-cpu
, where 42.0\% of qwen3:14b's tasks % reg: x2-evalset-tasks-real-v2-wrong-rate
and 80.4\% of qwen3:8b's task turns % reg: pkg1109-t4-8b
went silently wrong. Memory runs out silently too, and not where the reported figures say (Section~\ref{sec:4.4}).

Five lessons follow. Set the window explicitly for each device and verify the loaded value% reg: window-override-methods
. Surface the runtime's cut-off log lines (Ollama writes them only at debug level~\cite{ollama14259}), but do not treat them as a correctness check. Try a quantized KV cache before a smaller window: on the AMD APU it cut session time by 85\% (37.1 of 43.7 min) and 65\% (139.4 of 214.8 min, 3 sessions per setting) with no measured loss of rules or recall% reg: cost-cut-config-time | b5-kv-recall-f16-rerun
. Treat trimming as a change to the prompt: dropping old turns drops the examples the model imitates% reg: R2-qwen3-14b-4096-reconciliation
. Validate an agent on its target backend: identical model files and settings behaved differently on CUDA, Vulkan and ROCm% reg: blade-r2-gate-v2 | x2-r2-llama-val-32k-vulkan | x2-r2-llama-val-32k-offload
.
